Three ways to back up your home

Pick the one that fits how you live.

The Farmington Valley learned this in 2020: Tropical Storm Isaias left almost all of Avon without power, some homes for days.

Standby generator

Permanent, automatic, and runs for days on your gas line or propane tank. Most homes land between 14 and 26 kW. Most of ours are Briggs & Stratton.

  • Best for long outages, well or sump pumps, medical equipment
  • No one has to be home to switch it on
  • Transfer switch, pad, permit, startup and walkthrough

Home battery

A wall-mounted battery that takes over instantly and silently. Pairs well with solar, which recharges it during a long outage.

  • No fuel, no noise, no yearly engine service
  • Covers essentials for a day or more; whole-home needs more capacity
  • Connecticut’s Energy Storage Solutions program pays incentives

Portable generator inlet

A safe, code-compliant way to run your house from a portable generator. No extension cords through the window.

  • A fraction of the cost of the other two
  • Outdoor inlet plus a panel interlock, so you can’t back-feed the grid
  • Cheapest when done with a panel upgrade

Questions

Backup power FAQ

Not sure which way to go? Call or text and we’ll talk it through.

Generator or battery?

A generator wins for long outages and whole-house loads like central AC. A battery wins on noise, upkeep and short outages, and it’s the natural fit if you have solar. Some homes do both: a battery for blips, a generator for the big storms.

How big a generator do I need?

It depends on what you want running. Essentials (heat, fridge, sump pump, some lights and outlets) need a smaller unit than the whole house with central AC. We’ll make the list with you.

How much does it cost?

In 2026, most standby generators we install run $XX,XXX–$XX,XXX installed, depending on size and the gas or propane run. An inlet and interlock for a portable runs $X,XXX. Battery prices depend mostly on capacity; we’ll quote it alongside a generator so you can compare.

How often does a generator need service?

Once a year for most homes. The unit exercises itself on a schedule, but oil, filters and the battery still need a person. That’s what the care plan is for.
