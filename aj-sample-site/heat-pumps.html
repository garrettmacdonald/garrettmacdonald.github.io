Heat pump & mini-split wiring

Going electric? Get the wiring right first.

A heat pump is the biggest electrical load most homes ever add. We check that your service can carry it, wire the outdoor and indoor units, and work around your HVAC installer’s schedule so install day goes smoothly.

The electrical side

What a heat pump needs from your panel.

  • A load calculation on your current service, so you know before you buy
  • A dedicated circuit and disconnect for each outdoor unit
  • Air handler and backup heat circuits for ducted systems (backup heat strips are big loads)
  • Circuits for mini-split heads where the system needs them
  • A service upgrade when the numbers say you need one
  • Permit and inspection for the electrical work

Already have an HVAC installer?

Good. We work alongside heat pump installers across the area. Send us their proposal or have them call us, and we’ll line up the electrical for their install day.

Rebates

Energize CT’s biggest heat pump rebates are paid through installers in its Heat Pump Installer Network, so ask your HVAC company. We make sure the electrical side is permitted and inspected, so it never holds up the paperwork. (The federal 25C tax credit ended December 31, 2025.) CT rebates, 2026

Questions

Heat pump wiring FAQ

Will I need a panel upgrade for a heat pump?

Often, on a 100 A house, but not always. It depends on the heat pump size, the backup heat, and what else is on the panel. The load calculation answers it.

Who wires the thermostat?

Usually your HVAC installer handles the low-voltage controls. We handle the power: circuits, disconnects and the panel.

Can you wire mini-splits in several rooms?

Yes. Multi-zone systems usually run from one outdoor unit on one circuit; several single-zone units each need their own. We’ll check the panel can take them.
